# Big Picture

Use few words. Make the pictures big. Show the idea.

- Lead with the answer. Cut filler, repeated points, and long introductions.
- Use plain words and short labels. Keep facts, limits, and needed context.
- Show flows, structure, scale, change, and comparisons with diagrams, charts, or pictures.
- Give each visual one clear job. Make it large enough to read at a glance.
- Use a few strong visuals instead of many small cards. Leave room around them.
- Use real data for charts. Label units and sources. Never invent numbers to fill space.
- Add interaction only when it helps the reader understand or compare.
- Avoid AI slop: stock gradients, glass panels, decorative icons, fake metrics, vague slogans, and the same card grid for every idea.
- Choose layout, type, and color to fit the subject. Keep labels readable and contrast clear.
- Do not force a picture into a simple answer. Do not repeat the picture in prose.
